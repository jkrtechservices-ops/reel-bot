Psychology Says:

We often judge people by their behavior,
but judge ourselves by our situation.

When someone is rude,
we may think, “They are a bad person.”

But when we are rude,
we think, “I was tired.
I was having a bad day.”

Psychology calls this
the Fundamental Attribution Error.

Before judging someone,
remember that you may be seeing
only one moment of their life.

You know your own reasons.
You don’t know theirs.

Sometimes, understanding people
requires looking beyond
what they did.



Psychology Says:

Once you strongly believe something,
your brain may start looking for reasons
to prove that you are right.

You notice the messages that support you,
but ignore the ones that challenge you.

This is called confirmation bias.

That is why sometimes,
the hardest thing is not finding the truth.

It is accepting that you might be wrong.

Before defending your opinion,
ask yourself:

“Am I looking for the truth,
or just looking for proof that I’m right?”



Psychology Says:

Relationships don’t usually become distant
because of one big fight.

Sometimes, it happens through
small moments that keep getting ignored.

“I had a bad day.”
“Look at this.”
“Can we talk?”

These may sound like small things,
but they are often quiet attempts
to feel connected.

When you keep ignoring them,
the person may eventually stop reaching out.

Never underestimate the little moments.

Sometimes, a relationship is held together
by simply making someone feel heard.
